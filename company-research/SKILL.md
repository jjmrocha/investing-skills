# Company Research

Assemble the evidence someone needs to understand a company. **Evidence only — never a verdict.**

The report is always complete, whether or not the reader already knows the company. Completeness is the mechanism: if every report covers the same ground, the parts the reader didn't already know are exactly the parts they were missing. Never ask what they already believe — just produce the whole thing.

## The two rules

**1. No verdicts.** Do not rate the moat, call the strategy sound, write a bull or bear case, name a price target, quote analyst consensus, or suggest buying or selling. Report what the company says, what the numbers show, what employees and customers say — and stop. The reader forms the view; handing them a conclusion is the one thing that destroys the skill's value.

**2. Every line in the body is sourced.** If you could not source it, it does not appear in the body at all — no blank field, no placeholder, no estimate, no "approximately" standing in for a number you didn't find. Drop the line and record it under *What I could not establish*. A field waiting to be filled is how invented figures get in.

Attribute to document and period — "FY2025 annual report, Legal Proceedings" — not to a page number. Page numbers are the citation detail most easily fabricated and least easily checked.

## Mandatory sources

Read all of these every time. Reaching none of them is a finding; skipping one silently is a defect. Anything you could not reach goes in the gaps section by name.

| Document type | US filer | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| Annual report **including the footnotes** | 10-K | Annual report / 20-F / local regulator filing |
| Latest interim report | 10-Q | Half-year or quarterly report where required |
| Last two results calls | Transcript or webcast | Same; often thinner or absent outside the US |
| Ownership disclosure | DEF 14A, 13F, 13D/G | Major-holdings notifications, share registry |
| Material events since the annual report | 8-K | Regulatory news service announcements |
| Employee sentiment | Glassdoor | Glassdoor, Indeed, local equivalents |
| Customer/product evidence | Reviews, G2, app stores, trade press | Same |

Jurisdiction sets the document names, not the shape of the work. See [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) for where each lives and how to search it.

## The footnotes are the point

The narrative sections are written by investor relations. The footnotes are written by lawyers and accountants, and they are where the things nobody quotes actually live:

- **Customer and supplier concentration** — often a single sentence revealing that three distributors are half of revenue
- **Litigation reserves** — and, more telling, the *movement* in the reserve year over year and the reason given
- **Segment detail** beyond the headline split
- **Revenue recognition or accounting-policy changes** that make this year incomparable to last
- **Contingencies, guarantees, related-party transactions, off-balance-sheet obligations**
- **Debt covenants and maturity walls**

When a reserve, a segment, or a policy moved materially, report the movement and the company's stated reason for it. That contrast — what changed versus how it's explained — is the highest-value content in the report.

## Procedure

1. Identify the company, its listing, and its filing regime. Find its filing home before searching anything else.
2. Read the latest annual report end to end, footnotes included.
3. Read the latest interim report.
4. Read or watch the last two results calls, prepared remarks and Q&A both.
5. Pull ownership disclosures.
6. Sweep material events and news since the annual report — roughly twelve months.
7. Read Glassdoor, then customer and product reviews.
8. Write the report using [references/template.md](references/template.md).
9. Collect every unanswered item into *What I could not establish*.

Deliver the report in the conversation. Do not write it to a file unless asked.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Filling a field with an estimate because the template has a slot for it | Drop the line. Record it as a gap. |
| Citing a page number | Cite document and section. |
| Stopping at the press release or the news coverage | The press release is the company's summary of itself. Read the filing. |
| Drifting into a verdict — "strong moat", "well positioned", "attractive" | Report the evidence that prompted the adjective and delete the adjective. |
| Treating an empty gaps section as success | An empty list usually means you didn't look hard enough. A long one is a real finding about disclosure quality. |
| Reporting a headline number without the footnote that qualifies it | Segment revenue, reserves and margins routinely carry qualifications that reverse the headline reading. |
