# Report Template

Fill only the lines you can source. **Delete any line you cannot source** — do not leave it blank and do not estimate. Every deleted line becomes an entry in *What I could not establish*.

No verdicts anywhere: no moat rating, no bull or bear case, no price target, no analyst consensus, no recommendation.

```markdown
# [Company] — Research Report
**Pulled:** [YYYY-MM-DD] · **Primary sources:** [annual report FY####, interim report, results calls, ownership filings]
*Point-in-time. Figures are as filed on the pull date.*

## TL;DR
Ten lines of fact, no conclusions. What the company does, how it makes money, scale,
who owns it, the two or three numbers that matter, and the risks the company itself names.

## What the company does
- **Legal name / listing:** [Name, exchange:ticker]
- **Headquarters:** []
- **Founded:** []
- **Employees:** [count, as disclosed]
- **Sector:** []

[Two or three sentences describing the actual business in plain terms.]

## Products and services
| Line | What it is | Scale |
|---|---|---|

## Customers
Who buys, and in what concentration. Quote the concentration disclosure verbatim
where one exists — it is usually a single sentence in the footnotes.

## Geography
Revenue by region as disclosed. Note where the company's reporting regions
are broader than the geography actually driving the numbers.

## History
Founding, and the events that made the company what it is now — not a full chronology.
Separations, transformative acquisitions, near-death moments, regime changes.

## Ownership
- **Institutional total:** [%]
- **Largest holders:** [name, %, as-of date, filing type]
- **Insider / founder / family / state holdings:** []
- **Dual-class or control structure:** []

## Recent developments
Material events since the last annual report, and the significant ones from the
year before it. Acquisitions, divestitures, leadership changes, litigation turns,
regulatory decisions. Date each one.

## Business model and competitive position
**How revenue is actually earned**, per line: mechanism, margin profile where disclosed,
what it depends on.

**Named competitors** — the company's own list from the annual report, plus who it
is measured against in practice.

**Evidence bearing on durability.** Facts only, no rating: switching costs visible
in disclosures, pricing power visible in margins, patent or exclusivity expiries with
dates, regulatory barriers, scale economics, what management claims and what the
employee and customer evidence says about it.

## Strategy
What management says it is doing, sourced to the call or the annual report, and where
the capital actually went — R&D, capex, acquisitions, buybacks, dividends. Note where
the two disagree.

## Risks
The company's own disclosed risks first, prioritised by what the disclosure movement
suggests matters — a reserve that moved, language that changed year over year, a new
risk factor. Then observable risks the filings do not frame as risks.

Each entry: what it is, the disclosure it comes from, and any figure the company puts on it.

## What employees say
Glassdoor rating, review count, as-of date. Recurring themes in both directions.
Note whether the trend is moving.

## What customers say
Review sources, ratings, recurring themes. Say plainly if the company has no
reviewable consumer surface.

## What I could not establish
Every question left open, and every mandatory source not reached.

- [Question] — [what was searched, why it failed]
- [Source not reached] — [which one, why]
```
