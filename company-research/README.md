# company-research

Everything you'd want to know about a company, gathered from primary sources — and nothing about what to think of it.

## What it does

Produces one full report on a company: what it does, what it sells, who buys, where it operates, its history, who owns it, what's happened lately, how it actually makes money, what management says the strategy is, what the evidence says about durability, and what the risks are.

The report is always complete, whether or not you already know the company. That's the point. If every report covers the same ground, then for a company you know well, the parts you didn't already know are exactly the parts you were missing — so the skill never asks what you already believe, it just produces the whole thing and lets the gaps show themselves.

It reads the annual report **including the footnotes**, the latest interim report, the last two results calls, ownership filings, a year of material events, Glassdoor, and customer reviews. The footnotes are where the things nobody quotes live: customer concentration, litigation reserves and their movement, accounting-policy changes that make this year incomparable to last.

## When it triggers

"Research company X", "what does X actually do", "what am I missing about X" — due diligence, competitive intelligence, or any point before you form a view on a business.

## What it deliberately isn't

**It won't tell you what to think.** No moat rating, no bull or bear case, no price target, no analyst consensus, no buy or sell. It brings the evidence a moat argument would be built from and stops there — because handing you the conclusion is the one thing that would destroy its value. The judgment is yours.

**It won't invent.** Any line it can't source is deleted rather than filled, and every deleted line reappears in a *What I could not establish* section at the end. A template field waiting to be filled is how fake numbers get into research reports; this one has no waiting fields. An unusually long gaps list is itself a finding — that company is opaque.

**It isn't US-only.** Organised by document type — annual report, interim report, results call, ownership disclosure — with 10-K and 10-Q as one jurisdiction's names for those things rather than the frame.
